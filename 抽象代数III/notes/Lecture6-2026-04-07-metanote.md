[sec]: Endomorphism rings, semisimple rings, and Artin--Wedderburn

[thm]: Let
\[
{}_RV = V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_n
\]
be a direct sum of $R$-modules, and suppose all $V_i$ are isomorphic to each other.
Then
\[
\operatorname{End}_R({}_RV) \cong M_n\bigl(\operatorname{End}_R(V_i)\bigr).
\]

[pf]: Choose fixed identifications $V_i \cong V$.
For $\alpha \in \operatorname{End}_R(U)$, use the projections $p_i : U \to V_i$ and inclusions $\iota_j : V_j \to U$ to form the matrix entries
\[
p_i \alpha \iota_j \in \operatorname{End}_R(V).
\]
Check this gives a ring homomorphism into the full matrix ring, and construct the inverse by starting from a matrix of endomorphisms and assembling an endomorphism of $U$.

[def]: A ring $R$ is called semisimple if ${}_R R$ is semisimple as a left $R$-module.

[lemma]: For a unital ring $R$, the following are equivalent:
(1) $R$ is semisimple.
(2) Every left $R$-module is semisimple.

[pf]: If ${}_R R$ is semisimple, then every free module is semisimple, and every module is a quotient of a free module.
Use closure properties of semisimple modules to deduce that every left $R$-module is semisimple.
The converse is immediate by applying the statement to ${}_R R$ itself.

[thm]: (Artin--Wedderburn) Let $A$ be a semisimple Artinian ring.
Then
\[
A \cong M_{n_1}(D_1) \oplus \cdots \oplus M_{n_r}(D_r),
\]
where each $D_i$ is a division ring.
More concretely, if
\[
{}_A A \cong S_1^{\oplus n_1} \oplus \cdots \oplus S_r^{\oplus n_r}
\]
with pairwise nonisomorphic simple $A$-modules $S_i$, then one may take
\[
D_i = \operatorname{End}_A(S_i).
\]

[pf]: Decompose $\operatorname{End}_A(A)$ using the decomposition of the regular module.
Use
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_A(S_i,S_j)=0
\quad (i \neq j)
\]
and Schur's lemma to identify the diagonal blocks with division rings.
Then use the previous matrix-ring result to rewrite
\[
\operatorname{End}_A\bigl(S_i^{\oplus n_i}\bigr)
\]
as
\[
M_{n_i}\bigl(\operatorname{End}_A(S_i)\bigr).
\]

[sec]: $k$-algebras and Schur-type consequences over algebraically closed fields

[def]: Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1$, and let $A$ be an associative ring with $1$.
An $R$-algebra is an $R$-module $A$ whose multiplication is compatible with the $R$-action.
Equivalently, there is a ring homomorphism
\[
R \to A
\]
whose image lies in the center $Z(A)$.

[rmk]: If $R=k$ is a field and $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$, then $A$ is called a finite-dimensional $k$-algebra.
Such an algebra is Noetherian and Artinian as a ring/module object.

[ex]: The examples in the source appear to include quotient algebras such as $R[x]/I$, matrix algebras $M_n(R)$, and group algebras attached to finite groups.
OCR is noisy on the exact list and notation, so please restore the exact examples from the PDF.

[lemma]: Let $A$ be a $k$-algebra, where $k$ is a field, and let $S$ be a simple $A$-module.
Then $\operatorname{End}_A(S)$ is a division ring, and it is naturally a $k$-algebra.

[lemma]: If $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$, then $\operatorname{End}_A(S)$ is finite-dimensional over $k$.

[lemma]: If $A$ is finite-dimensional over $k$ and $k$ is algebraically closed, then
\[
\operatorname{End}_A(S) = k.
\]

[pf]: Regard $S$ as a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space.
Then $\operatorname{End}_A(S)$ is a $k$-subspace of $\operatorname{End}_k(S)$.
For any
\[
f \in \operatorname{End}_A(S),
\]
choose an eigenvalue $\lambda \in k$.
Then $f-\lambda \operatorname{id}_S$ is not invertible, so it has nonzero kernel.
Because the kernel is an $A$-submodule of the simple module $S$, it must be all of $S$.
Hence $f=\lambda \operatorname{id}_S$.

[thm]: If $k$ is algebraically closed and $A$ is a finite-dimensional semisimple $k$-algebra, then
\[
A \cong M_{n_1}(k) \oplus \cdots \oplus M_{n_r}(k).
\]

[cor]: If
\[
{}_A A \cong \bigoplus_{i=1}^r S_i^{\oplus n_i}
\]
with the $S_i$ pairwise nonisomorphic simple $A$-modules, then
\[
S_1,\dots,S_r
\]
form a complete set of representatives for the isomorphism classes of simple $A$-modules.

[sec]: $k$-algebras, indecomposables, and idempotents

[def]: A nonzero $R$-module $M$ is called indecomposable if every decomposition
\[
M = M_1 \oplus M_2
\]
forces $M_1=0$ or $M_2=0$.

[thm]: (Krull--Schmidt) Let $M$ be an $R$-module with a composition series.
Suppose
\[
M \cong V_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus V_m
\cong
U_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus U_n
\]
where all $V_i$ and $U_j$ are indecomposable.
Then
\[
m=n,
\]
and after reordering the summands one has
\[
V_i \cong U_i
\quad\text{for all } i.
\]

[def]: An idempotent of a ring $R$ is a nonzero element
\[
e \in R
\]
such that
\[
e^2=e.
\]

[rmk]: If $e$ is an idempotent, then the corner ring
\[
eRe
\]
is a ring with identity element $e$.

[def]: Two idempotents $e_1,e_2 \in R$ are called orthogonal if
\[
e_1 e_2 = e_2 e_1 = 0.
\]
More generally, finitely many idempotents are orthogonal if they are pairwise orthogonal.

[lemma]: If $e_1,\dots,e_n$ are orthogonal idempotents, then
\[
e_1+\cdots+e_n
\]
is again an idempotent.

[def]: An idempotent decomposition of an idempotent $e$ is an expression
\[
e=e_1+\cdots+e_n
\]
where the $e_i$ are orthogonal idempotents.

[def]: An idempotent is called primitive if it cannot be written as a sum of two nonzero orthogonal idempotents.

[def]: A primitive orthogonal decomposition is an idempotent decomposition in which all summands are primitive.

[ex]: If $e$ is an idempotent in a unital ring, then
\[
1=e+(1-e)
\]
is an idempotent decomposition of $1$.

[thm]: Let $e$ be an idempotent of $R$.
If
\[
e=e_1+\cdots+e_n
\]
is an idempotent decomposition, then
\[
Re = Re_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus Re_n
\]
as a direct sum of left ideals / left $R$-modules.

[pf]: Every element of $Re$ can be written as
\[
ae = ae_1+\cdots+ae_n.
\]
Orthogonality of the $e_i$ gives directness of the sum.

[thm]: Conversely, if
\[
Re = I_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus I_n
\]
is a direct sum decomposition by left ideals, then there exist idempotents $e_1,\dots,e_n$ such that
\[
I_i = Re_i
\quad\text{and}\quad
e=e_1+\cdots+e_n.
\]

[pf]: Write
\[
e=a_1+\cdots+a_n
\]
with $a_i \in I_i$.
Then one checks that the $a_i$ are pairwise orthogonal idempotents and that
\[
I_i = Ra_i.
\]
Use these $a_i$ as the required $e_i$.

[cor]: For an idempotent $e \in R$, the following are equivalent:
(1) $e$ is primitive.
(2) $Re$ is indecomposable as a left $R$-module.
(3) The corner ring $eRe$ has no nontrivial idempotents, i.e. its only idempotents are $0$ and $e$.

The third condition above is the standard statement and seems to match the OCR fragment "e is the unique idempotent of eRe", but please verify the exact wording in the PDF.
