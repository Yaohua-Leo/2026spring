# 03_why_jordan_algebras_appeared
[Transition] This brings us to why jordan algebras appeared.
Jordan algebras were born from quantum mechanics. In the usual Copenhagen model, physical observables are represented by self-adjoint, or Hermitian, matrices or operators. But the ordinary matrix structure is not itself fully observable: for example, the product of two Hermitian operators is not necessarily Hermitian. Pascual Jordan therefore proposed a new algebraic setting that would depend only on observable operations, and not on the whole invisible matrix structure behind them.
Key points: 1. Quantum mechanics in the Copenhagen model 2. Observables = Hermitian matrices or operators 3. Ordinary matrix operations are not all observable
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 04_jordan_s_new_product
[Transition] This brings us to jordan's new product.
Jordan's key idea was to replace ordinary multiplication with the symmetrized product x o y = 1/2 (xy + yx), sometimes called quasi-multiplication. This operation is commutative, and more importantly, it sends Hermitian elements to Hermitian elements. So it captures an observable product on the space of observables. This simple formula became the starting point of the whole theory of Jordan algebras.
Key points: 1. Quasi-multiplication: 2. x o y = 1/2 (xy + yx) 3. Commutative
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 05_basic_definition
[Transition] This brings us to basic definition.
Formally, a Jordan algebra is a commutative algebra satisfying the Jordan identity. In the original physical setting, Jordan and his collaborators were especially interested in formally real Jordan algebras, where a sum of squares can vanish only when each term is zero. The point was to isolate the algebraic behavior of observables in quantum mechanics and then ask what other systems satisfy the same laws.
Key points: 1. A Jordan algebra is a commutative algebra 2. It satisfies the Jordan identity 3. (x^2 o y) o x = x^2 o (y o x)
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 06_the_first_basic_examples
[Transition] This brings us to the first basic examples.
The first examples are very natural. Any associative algebra gives a Jordan algebra if we symmetrize the product, and this is denoted by A+. If the associative algebra has an involution, then its Hermitian part H(A, *) is again a Jordan algebra. Another important family is given by spin factors, where vectors multiply through the inner product. These examples already show that Jordan theory is broader than just Hermitian matrices.
Key points: 1. A+ : any associative algebra with symmetrized product 2. H(A, *) : Hermitian elements under an involution 3. Spin factors:
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 07_special_and_exceptional
[Transition] This brings us to special and exceptional.
A central distinction in the subject is the difference between special and exceptional Jordan algebras. A special Jordan algebra can be realized inside some symmetrized associative algebra A+. An exceptional Jordan algebra cannot. Jordan originally hoped that exceptional algebras would come in infinite families, and that such families might provide a new home for quantum mechanics beyond the usual matrix model.
Key points: 1. Special: comes from an associative algebra 2. Exceptional: does not come from any A+ 3. Jordan hoped for infinite families of exceptional algebras
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 08_the_1934_classification
[Transition] This brings us to the 1934 classification.
In their fundamental 1934 paper, Jordan, von Neumann, and Wigner classified finite-dimensional formally real Jordan algebras. They showed that the simple building blocks are Hermitian matrix algebras over the real numbers, complex numbers, and quaternions, together with spin factors and one octonionic case, namely H_3(O). This result was a major milestone, because it sharply limited what exceptional examples could look like.
Key points: 1. Jordan-von Neumann-Wigner classification 2. Finite-dimensional formally real simple algebras: 3. H_n(R)
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 09_the_albert_algebra
[Transition] This brings us to the albert algebra.
The most famous exceptional Jordan algebra is H_3(O), the Jordan algebra of 3x3 Hermitian octonionic matrices. It has dimension 27, and Albert proved that it is genuinely exceptional. Today it is called the Albert algebra. Historically, this was both exciting and disappointing: exciting because it revealed a truly new structure, but disappointing because it seemed to be only one isolated example rather than a large family.
Key points: 1. H_3(O) has dimension 27 2. A. A. Albert proved it is exceptional 3. Now called the Albert algebra
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 10_from_physics_to_pure_algebra
[Transition] This brings us to from physics to pure algebra.
After the initial physical motivation faded, Jordan theory was taken up by algebraists. The subject developed its own structure theory and its own methods, especially through work associated with Albert and Jacobson, and later through Meyberg, Loos, McCrimmon, and many others. McCrimmon's survey presents this development as a long historical arc from the origins in quantum mechanics to Zelmanov's decisive classification results.
Key points: 1. The subject moved beyond physics 2. Structure theory became central 3. Major names: Albert, Jacobson, Meyberg, Loos, McCrimmon
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 11_beyond_the_real_case
[Transition] This brings us to beyond the real case.
A major step was the move from the real, formally real setting to Jordan algebras over algebraically closed fields and then over arbitrary fields. In this broader setting, one distinguishes split, reduced, and division forms. Albert algebras also appear in these different forms, not only in the classical real octonionic picture. This made the theory much richer and more algebraic.
Key points: 1. Study over algebraically closed fields and general fields 2. Split, reduced, and division forms 3. Albert algebras over general fields
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 12_the_quadratic_viewpoint
[Transition] This brings us to the quadratic viewpoint.
One of the deepest conceptual changes in the subject was the shift from the bilinear product x o y to the quadratic operator U_x(y). In special Jordan algebras, this operator corresponds to the familiar associative expression xyx. This quadratic viewpoint made inverses, isotopes, and structure theory much clearer, and it also helped extend Jordan theory beyond the older framework that depended strongly on dividing by 2.
Key points: 1. The quadratic operator: 2. U_x(y) = 2x o (x o y) - x^2 o y 3. In special algebras: U_x(y) = xyx
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 13_the_wider_jordan_family
[Transition] This brings us to the wider jordan family.
As the theory matured, it became clear that Jordan algebras are only one member of a larger family of Jordan structures. Jordan triples, Jordan pairs, and Jordan superalgebras all emerged as natural generalizations. This widening of the subject was closely related both to Lie theory and to geometry, and it showed that Jordan algebra was not an isolated construction but part of a much broader mathematical framework.
Key points: 1. Jordan algebras 2. Jordan triples 3. Jordan pairs
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 14_links_with_lie_theory
[Transition] This brings us to links with lie theory.
Jordan theory is closely linked with Lie theory. Through the Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction, Jordan systems can be used as coordinates for certain graded Lie algebras. The exceptional Albert algebra is especially important here, because it is related to exceptional Lie groups and Lie algebras. So Jordan algebras became valuable not only in their own right, but also as a way to understand structures in Lie theory.
Key points: 1. Jordan systems as coordinates for graded Lie algebras 2. Tits-Kantor-Koecher construction 3. Exceptional Jordan algebra and exceptional Lie groups
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 15_links_with_geometry_and_analysis
[Transition] This brings us to links with geometry and analysis.
Jordan algebras also have deep geometric and analytic applications. They appear in the study of symmetric cones and bounded symmetric domains, where the algebraic structure encodes geometric information. In infinite-dimensional settings, Jordan operator algebras and JB-algebras connect the subject with functional analysis. The Albert algebra also plays an important role in projective geometry, especially in the description of octonion planes.
Key points: 1. Symmetric cones 2. Bounded symmetric domains 3. JB-algebras and functional analysis
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 16_the_long_open_problem
[Transition] This brings us to the long open problem.
For a long time, the central open problem was whether there existed further simple exceptional Jordan algebras, especially in infinite dimensions. The Albert algebra was known, but it was only 27-dimensional and too small for the original physical hopes. So the big question was whether Jordan's dream might survive in a larger exceptional setting. This question remained open for roughly half a century.
Key points: 1. Was the Albert algebra the only exceptional simple example? 2. Could there be infinite-dimensional exceptional Jordan algebras? 3. This question remained open for decades
Duration: 1.0 min

---
# 17_zelmanov_s_breakthrough
[Transition] This brings us to zelmanov's breakthrough.
This brings me to Efim Zelmanov. His theorem from 1983 says that every simple Jordan algebra, even in arbitrary dimension, falls into one of only three classes: Hermitian type, spin or Clifford type, or Albert type. In other words, no new simple exceptional Jordan algebras exist beyond the Albert case. This is the decisive structural theorem in the history of Jordan algebras.
Key points: 1. Theorem (Zelmanov, 1983): any simple Jordan algebra is: 2. 1. Hermitian type 3. 2. Spin / Clifford type
Duration: 1.3 min

---
# 18_why_zelmanov_matters_so_much
[Transition] This brings us to why zelmanov matters so much.
Zelmanov's contribution matters for two reasons. First, it gave a final answer to the historical question that started the subject: there is no infinite-dimensional exceptional simple setting for quantum mechanics. Second, it provided a global structural picture of simple Jordan algebras. McCrimmon explicitly presents the historical survey of Jordan theory as leading up to this result, and also notes that Zelmanov's work here later played a role in the broader context of his Fields Medal achievements on the Burnside problem.
Key points: 1. Closed the original search for exceptional quantum mechanics 2. Showed Albert algebras are the only simple exceptional case 3. Gave a final large-scale structural picture
Duration: 1.4 min

---
# 19_a_modern_direction_connected_with_sustech
[Transition] This brings us to a modern direction connected with sustech.
To end on a modern note, let me mention a direction represented in the SUSTech-affiliated talk by Iryna Kashuba. The idea is to study all n-dimensional complex Jordan algebras as points of an algebraic variety Jor_n, with change of basis given by the action of GL(n, C). In this setting one studies deformations, rigid algebras, and more recently moment maps and Jordan solitons. This is a beautiful example of how Jordan theory continues to develop through interactions with algebraic geometry and geometric invariant theory.
Key points: 1. Iryna Kashuba (SICM-SUSTech): varieties of Jordan algebras 2. Jor_n: the affine variety of n-dimensional complex Jordan algebras 3. GL(n, C)-orbits = isomorphism classes
Duration: 1.0 min
