
\begin{remark}[Maschke's theorem]
    Let \(G\) be a finite group, and let \(K\) be a field such that
    \[
        \operatorname{char}(K)\nmid |G|.
    \]
    Then every \(K[G]\)-module is completely reducible.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}[Idea of the proof]
    It suffices to show that every submodule \(W\) of a \(K[G]\)-module \(V\)
    is a direct summand.

    Choose a \(K\)-linear projection
    \[
        P\colon V\to W
    \]
    (which need not commute with the action of \(G\)). Averaging over the group,
    define
    \[
        P'=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G} gPg^{-1}.
    \]
    Then \(P'\) commutes with the action of \(G\). Moreover, \(P'\) still maps
    \(V\) into \(W\), and for every \(w\in W\) one has \(P'(w)=w\). Hence
    \[
        \operatorname{Im}(P')=W.
    \]
    Therefore \(\ker P'\) is a \(G\)-submodule and
    \[
        V=W\oplus \ker P'.
    \]
    So \(W\) is a direct summand. It follows that \(V\) is completely reducible.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation with Weyl's theorem]
    Weyl's original proof of complete reducibility uses the same averaging idea
    for representations of compact Lie groups, replacing the finite sum by an
    integral over the group (with respect to Haar measure).
\end{remark}
